Software decision guide

Do freelancers need a CRM?

Not always. You need a reliable way to remember prospects, follow up, and see what happens next. A CRM can provide that system, but a simple spreadsheet or task board may be enough while conversations are few.

Start with the workflow, not the software

Write down how a new enquiry moves through your business: record it, qualify the work, prepare a proposal, follow up on a specific date, and mark the outcome. If you can do this consistently in one simple system, you may not need a CRM yet.

Signs a CRM may help

  • You regularly forget to follow up with promising leads.
  • You cannot quickly tell which enquiries are active.
  • You manage repeat clients, referrals, or several services with different sales steps.
  • You need a record of past conversations before writing a proposal.

When to keep your current system

Keep it simple if you have a small number of enquiries, your offer is still changing, or you have not yet defined the stages from enquiry to decision. Software cannot create a sales process for you.

A practical first test

For two weeks, track every enquiry in one place. Record the name, service requested, next action, and follow-up date. If the system is difficult to maintain, you now have a clear requirements list for a CRM trial.
