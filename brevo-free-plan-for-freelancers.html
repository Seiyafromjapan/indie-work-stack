Brevo free plan for freelancers: what to check

Brevo's free plan offers a large contact allowance, but its daily sending cap changes how bigger campaigns work. Test the complete signup-to-email workflow before deciding whether those limits fit your audience.

Published September 8, 2026 · By Seiya Utsumi

Disclosure: We have applied to Brevo's partner program. This page currently uses no affiliate link. Plan details can change; confirm them on Brevo's official pages before choosing.

The short answer

Brevo's Free plan is a practical test environment for a freelancer with a small audience or occasional campaigns. It includes 300 email sends per day, storage for up to 100,000 contacts, one user seat, basic reporting, and automation for up to 2,000 unique contacts. The daily limit, Brevo branding, and reporting limits are the main reasons you may eventually need a paid plan.

What the 300-per-day limit means

Brevo counts one email sent to one recipient as one send. Unused daily sends do not roll over. If you send a campaign to more than 300 contacts, only part of the audience receives it that day. Brevo's documentation says you must resume the suspended campaign after the quota resets to reach the remaining contacts.

This limit is easy to work with when your list is small. It becomes operationally awkward when a time-sensitive announcement must reach more than 300 people at once.

What you can test without paying

  • Create email campaigns with the drag-and-drop editor.
  • Save reusable email templates and sections.
  • Send marketing and transactional emails within the shared allowance.
  • Build a basic contact list and signup workflow.
  • Try active automations with up to 2,000 unique contacts.
  • Review basic campaign reports.

Run one complete test with your own address: submit the form, confirm the subscription, receive the promised resource, and verify that the contact enters the intended list or automation.

When the free plan fits

  • Your audience is below 300 people, or campaigns can be spread over several days.
  • You are validating a lead magnet or newsletter before investing.
  • One person manages the account.
  • Basic reports are enough for the first stage.
  • Visible Brevo branding is acceptable while you test.

When to consider upgrading or choosing another tool

  • A campaign must reach more than 300 recipients on the same day.
  • Your automations need to accept more than 2,000 unique contacts.
  • You need multiple users, advanced reporting, or less provider branding.
  • The paid tier required for your sending volume no longer fits the economics of your newsletter.

Write down the exact problem before upgrading. A limit is relevant only when it interrupts a real workflow.

A seven-day evaluation

  1. Define one job, such as delivering a checklist and sending one welcome message.
  2. Create a small test list rather than importing every contact.
  3. Build the form, confirmation step, and first email.
  4. Test with at least two addresses and check mobile display.
  5. Confirm unsubscribe handling and contact export.
  6. Review which reports are available on the free plan.
  7. Compare the first paid tier with your expected list and sending volume.
