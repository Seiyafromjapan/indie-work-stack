Kit’s free plan for freelancers: what can you actually do?

Kit’s free plan can cover the essentials of starting an email audience, but its limits matter once you want automated nurturing.

Published and fact-checked September 5, 2026 · By Seiya Utsumi

Editorial note: This guide uses Kit’s official pricing and help documentation. It is not a hands-on performance test. No affiliate links are included.

The short answer

Kit’s Free plan costs $0 per month for up to 10,000 subscribers. It includes unlimited forms, landing pages, and email broadcasts, plus tagging and segmentation. Email sequences, visual automations, rules, and RSS campaigns require a paid plan.

What the Free plan includes

  • up to 10,000 active email subscribers;
  • unlimited landing pages and opt-in forms;
  • unlimited one-time email broadcasts;
  • subscriber tags and segments;
  • digital products and subscriptions;
  • A/B testing for broadcasts, API access, and Free Recommendations.

These features are enough to publish a signup page, collect permission-based subscribers, organize them by source or interest, and send newsletters without a monthly software fee.

What still requires a paid plan

The Free plan does not include the full automation layer. Kit lists visual automations, email sequences, rules, RSS campaigns, Kit MCP, Smart Recommendations, and earning from Paid Recommendations among paid-plan features.

The practical dividing lineUse Free for forms and one-time broadcasts. Consider Creator when a subscriber should automatically receive a planned series of emails or move through a behavior-based workflow.

Who can start with Free

  • A freelancer testing whether readers will join an email list.
  • A consultant sending a periodic newsletter manually.
  • A creator who needs a landing page before building a full website.
  • A solo business that can manage sales follow-up separately.

If your immediate bottleneck is tracking proposals and individual prospects, an email platform may still be the wrong first tool. Read Kit vs. a CRM for freelancers before setting up both systems.

Three limits to plan for

  1. 10,000 subscribers: you will need to upgrade to keep sending after exceeding the Free limit.
  2. No automated sequences: a multi-email welcome course needs a paid plan.
  3. Creator Network enrollment: Kit says Free accounts are enrolled when they sign up, although it can be turned off in settings.

A low-risk way to evaluate Kit

  1. Define one audience and one reason to subscribe.
  2. Create one landing page or inline form.
  3. Use a tag to record the signup source.
  4. Send two or three useful broadcasts manually.
  5. Upgrade only when automation solves a repeated task you already understand.

Bottom line

Kit’s Free plan is substantial enough to validate an email-list strategy. Its main constraint is workflow automation, not basic audience collection or broadcasting. Check Kit’s current pricing before choosing a plan because limits and features can change.
