Editorial standards

How Indie Work Stack researches and updates its guides

Our goal is to help independent professionals make software decisions from clear criteria, current evidence, and honest limits.

Last updated September 6, 2026

Who publishes this site

Indie Work Stack is published by Seiya Utsumi for freelancers, consultants, creators, and solo service businesses evaluating practical software.

How we choose topics

We start with a specific decision a reader needs to make, such as whether to adopt a CRM, which email platform fits a workflow, or when a paid plan becomes useful. We prioritize topics where product categories overlap, pricing is difficult to compare, or marketing claims can obscure the practical trade-offs.

How we research

Factual product claims are checked against first-party sources whenever possible, including official pricing pages, product documentation, help centers, and published terms. We distinguish documented capabilities from our analysis of who may benefit from them.

If an article is based on documentation rather than hands-on testing, we say so. We do not claim personal use, performance testing, or results that have not occurred.

How we compare products

Comparisons begin with the reader’s workflow. We examine limits, required features, data portability, ongoing cost, and the work needed to maintain a system. A product can be a strong choice for one type of business and unnecessary for another.

Prices and feature limits can change. Dated articles show when key facts were last checked and link to official sources where readers can confirm current details.

Affiliate relationships

Some links may become affiliate links, meaning Indie Work Stack could receive a commission without changing the reader’s price. Commercial relationships do not determine whether a product receives favorable coverage. Relevant relationships are disclosed near the recommendation and governed by our Affiliate Disclosure.

Corrections and updates

We correct material errors when identified and update time-sensitive claims when practical. Readers can report an error through the contact address on the About page. A correction may include revising the article, updating its fact-check date, or clarifying the limits of the available evidence.

Use of analytics

Aggregate analytics can help us see which guides readers find and which links they use. Analytics data does not replace editorial judgment, and readers can decline analytics storage through the site notice. See the Privacy Policy for details.
