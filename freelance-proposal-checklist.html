Freelance proposal checklist

A proposal should make the decision easy to understand. Before sending, verify the scope, schedule, price, responsibilities, and exact next step.

Published and fact-checked September 6, 2026 · By Seiya Utsumi

The five-minute review

Confirm that the client can quickly identify what will be delivered, what is excluded, when work happens, what it costs, and how to approve. Remove anything that distracts from those decisions.

1. Restate the client’s outcome

Open with the problem, result, or change the client described. This confirms that the proposal responds to their situation rather than presenting a generic service package.

2. Make the scope testable

Name the deliverables and boundaries. Include quantities, formats, revision rounds, or approval points when they matter. State excluded work when a reasonable reader could otherwise assume it is included.

3. Show how timing works

List the main stages, expected start, target completion, and the feedback needed from the client. Explain which dependencies can move the schedule so a target date is not mistaken for an unconditional guarantee.

4. Explain the full price

State the currency, payment schedule, taxes or expenses, payment terms, and proposal validity date. The reader should not have to combine information from several messages to understand the financial commitment.

5. Assign responsibilities

Identify who supplies files, access, content, feedback, and final approval. Clear responsibilities prevent delays and make later scope conversations easier.

6. End with one next step

Tell the client exactly how to approve, ask a question, or schedule the next conversation. Record a follow-up date in your lead tracker before sending.

After sending

Save the final version, note the proposal date and value, and follow the timing in the freelance lead follow-up guide. If the scope changes, issue a written revision rather than relying on scattered messages.

Keep the proposal focusedA useful proposal contains enough detail to make a decision and begin the work. More pages do not automatically create more clarity.
