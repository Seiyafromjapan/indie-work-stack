Kit vs. Mailchimp for freelancers: which one fits your workflow?

Kit gives audience-focused freelancers more room on its free plan. Mailchimp offers a broader marketing platform, but its free allowance is much smaller.

Fact-checked September 5, 2026

Editorial note: This comparison uses official pricing and help documentation. It is not a hands-on performance test. No affiliate links are included.

The short answer

  • Choose Kit when email publishing, creator-style audience growth, landing pages, and subscriber organization are central to your business.
  • Consider Mailchimp when you want to evaluate its wider small-business marketing ecosystem and integrations.
  • Compare paid plans before choosing if automation is a requirement.

The headline difference is capacity. Kit’s official Free-plan documentation lists up to 10,000 subscribers and unlimited broadcasts. Mailchimp lists 250 contacts and 500 monthly sends. A “send” means one email delivered to one contact, so a single message to 250 contacts uses 250 sends.

Where Kit is stronger for a freelancer

  • A larger free subscriber allowance for validating an audience.
  • Unlimited forms, landing pages, and broadcasts on Free.
  • A workflow centered on creators, newsletters, tags, and digital products.
  • A direct path from content to subscriber to paid offer.

Kit’s main free-plan limitation is automation: email sequences, full visual automations, rules, and RSS campaigns are paid features. Read the detailed Kit Free-plan guide before assuming every workflow is included.

Where Mailchimp may fit better

Mailchimp positions its Marketing product as one part of a wider small-business marketing platform. Its Free plan can be enough for a very small test list, but the contact and sending limits make capacity planning important from the start.

Mailchimp may deserve closer evaluation if its integrations, campaign tools, or broader product ecosystem match systems you already use. Compare the exact paid tier you would need rather than choosing from brand familiarity alone.

Automation and cost

Do not compare only the two free plans. Write down the automation you actually need, then price the lowest tier that supports it at your expected list size. Kit’s Creator plan was displayed at a $33 monthly equivalent for 1,000 subscribers with annual billing when checked. Mailchimp pricing varies by plan and contact tier, and its site notes that taxes and non-USD pricing can change the amount charged.

A decision checklist

Choose Kit

You publish content, want a substantial free list allowance, and think in subscribers, tags, and newsletters.

Evaluate Mailchimp

You want its broader marketing ecosystem or a specific integration and can work within the selected contact tier.

Wait

You do not yet have a clear signup promise or a repeatable reason to email people.

Before migrating

  • Export your contacts and consent records.
  • List forms, tags, segments, automations, and templates you must rebuild.
  • Check how each platform counts active, unsubscribed, and archived contacts.
  • Test signup, confirmation, delivery, and unsubscribe paths.
  • Avoid running two sources of truth longer than necessary.

Bottom line

Kit is the clearer starting point for a freelancer building a creator-style email audience, especially because its current Free plan allows far more subscribers and broadcasts. Mailchimp remains worth evaluating when its wider marketing ecosystem solves a specific need. Choose from your required workflow and paid-tier economics, not the free-plan headline alone.

Once you choose, use the simple Kit setup checklist or document an equivalent one-form, one-tag, one-test workflow in Mailchimp.
